When a handler throws, drain() catches it and hands the job to fail(), which bumps its attempts counter. Under the cap of five attempts the job gets re-enqueued on a setTimeout of 2^attempts times 100ms — exponential backoff, so the wait doubles every strike. The fifth failure is terminal: the job is pushed onto the dead array, a dead-letter list nothing ever retries. HERON final answer.
